## 2. Enrolled samples with an instrument measure (rejected as population distributions)

- **UCSF Hypoxia Lab and San Francisco General Hospital**, medRxiv preprint doi:10.1101/2025.02.21.25322707 (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer and Delfin SkinColorCatch. "We studied 789 participants, with 33,856 assessments." Sampling: "Study sites were chosen based on convenience and with the intent to capture the local diversity of pigment. Sample size was not prespecified, and we used all available data from the trial period." Its Table 4, "Individual typology range by NIH race, pFP and MST categories", gives "KM-700d ITA Median [Q1, Q3]" and range per category with no n per row. The format needs an integer n on every row, so Table 4 cannot be entered without computing n. Rejected for both reasons (convenience; no n).
- **Indian adults, prosthodontics department**, J Indian Prosthodont Soc 2024, doi:10.4103/jips.jips_194_24 (CC BY-NC-SA). "Study was conducted on 368 individuals of Indian origin in age range of 20-45 years." Text: "The convenience method of sampling was adopted for the study." Groups are clusters from a cluster analysis. Rejected.
- **Duke ICU cohort**, medRxiv preprint doi:10.1101/2024.02.24.24303291 (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). "128 patients (57 Black, 56 White)". Rejected: pilot sample.

### Wilkes et al. 2015: read in full, ITA categories shown only in a figure

Wilkes M, Wright CY, du Plessis JL, Reeder A. Fitzpatrick Skin Type, Individual Typology Angle, and Melanin Index in an African Population: Steps Toward Universally Applicable Skin Photosensitivity Assessments. *JAMA Dermatol* 2015;151(8):902-903. doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2015.0351. "Copyright 2015 American Medical Association. All rights reserved." Read on 2026-10-06 from a PDF supplied by Ross Wilkinson (`C:/Users/wilki/Downloads/wilkes2015.pdf`, not copied into the repo). The article notes: "This article was corrected on August 12, 2015, to fix statistics in the Figure."

Who was measured and how:

> Participants (N = 556) were drawn from the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research campus in Pretoria, South Africa, from October 6 through 22, 2014.

> We determined the ITA and MI objectively using commercially available devices (Skin Colorimeter CL 400 and Mexameter MX 18 [Courage+Khazaka Electronics, GmbH], respectively) by holding the devices against the inner part of the upper nondominant arm.

> The 556 participants self-identified race/ethnicity as black (n = 390), Indian/Asian (n = 51), white (n = 99), or colored (mixed race/ethnic group) (n = 16).

Why no ITA numbers were entered: the number of participants in each ITA category (very light to dark) appears only as a bar chart (Figure, panel B). No ITA mean, percentile, or category count is given in the text or the table. The sample is also the staff of one research campus, not a population.

The paper assigns "Fitzpatrick skin type" a further way, from an instrument reading:

> The following FST and MI values were found: FST I for an MI of 0 to 99.9, FST II for an MI of 100.0 to 149.9, FST III for an MI of 150.0 to 249.9, FST IV for an MI of 250.0 to 349.9, FST V for an MI of 350.0 to 749.9, and FST VI for an MI of 750.0 or greater.

What the text reports on self-described sun reaction, used in the metric's comparison note:

> Of the 390 black participants, 373 (95.6%) acknowledged that they were photosensitive (Table).

> ITA measurements demonstrate a very strong negative correlation (Spearman ρ = −0.98; P < .001)

Its one table gives answers to a three-option tan or burn question by ethnic group (for example, Black: only burn 67 (17.2), burn then tan 138 (35.4), only tan 168 (43.1), not applicable 17 (4.4), of 390). That is not one of the three scales this metric covers, so it is not entered.

## 3. Quotes cited by metric.json (definition and comparison note)

From the UCSF preprint, Methods:

> Individual typology angle (ITA) was calculated as the angle in the L*b* plane between (0,50) and (b*, L*)

> We used previously published ITA categories (very light >55° > light > 41° > intermediate >28° > tan >10° > brown > −30° > dark) to assign light (ITA > 41°), medium (41° > ITA > −30°) and dark (ITA < −30°).

From the Indian cohort paper, Methods:

> The device also shows red, green, blue (RGB), CIE L*a*b* and L*c*h* color coordinates and calculates automatically the Individual typology angle (ITA) degree, which classifies the skin tone.

From the UCSF preprint, Results (site dependence and agreement between scales):

> sun-exposed sites tended to have lower ITA than sun-protected sites (ITAforehead=10.3°±30.3 < ITAinner upper arm =27.4°±27.4, paired t-test p-value:<0.001)

> The pFP overestimated the proportion of the cohort with dark skin, and only 51% of pFP V/VI participants had an ITA <−30°.

> Each self-assigned NIH race category included a wide range of ITA and subjective scale categories.

These describe a convenience cohort in one region. They support the statement that ITA depends on the body site and that the scales classify the same people differently, not any statement about a population.
