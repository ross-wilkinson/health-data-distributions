Read this before using these numbers in your sample size calculations:
  • This is a clinic pulse, not a wearable resting heart rate. Overnight or daily resting heart rate from an optical wrist sensor is a different physiological quantity taken under different conditions. Do not use these numbers as a wearable reference.
  • General population, not a filtered "healthy" reference. Nobody was excluded for cardiovascular medication (e.g., beta-blockers), medical conditions, pregnancy, or arrhythmias.
  • Even numbers only. Because the protocol doubles a 30-second count, all observations and percentiles move in increments of 2 beats/min.

Measurement Method is Part of the Data

Why two datasets measuring "resting heart rate" cannot be merged.

Clinic Count

NHANES 2015–2018 Clinic Pulse

72.4 bpm

Population: Probability sample of US general population (n = 10,848, weighted, no exclusions for medication or conditions).

Protocol: Counted by trained examiner for 30s seated after 5 min quiet rest during a clinic exam visit.

Citation: CDC/NCHS public data, recomputed via survey Rao-Wu bootstrap.

Wearable Sensor

Quer et al. 2020 Fitbit Cohort

65.5 bpm

Population: Self-selected convenience sample of 92,457 device owners meeting rigorous wear-time criteria (63% women, avg age 45.8).

Protocol: Proprietary PPG algorithm during periods of sleep and supine waking; multi-day longitudinal subject mean.

Citation: Quer G, et al. Lancet Digital Health 2020;2(2):e58-e66.

Notice the 6.9 bpm gap between the two population means. Method, timing, posture, and population self-selection all differ. Merging these sources would produce an artifactual distribution that describes neither clinic pulse nor wearable monitoring.

The Invariants of Health Data Distributions

Every distribution in this repository satisfies strict epistemological rules.

Invariant 1
Every number is traceable
Recomputed numbers link directly to Python pipelines run on public data. Transcribed numbers include page, table, and verbatim quotes.
Invariant 4
Method is part of the data
Two sources for the same clinical metric are distinct distributions until proven otherwise. Never pool across disparate measurement protocols.
Invariant 3
Report what the source reports
Do not convert means and SDs into fabricated percentiles or impose Gaussian assumptions where real physiology has heavy tails.
Invariant 6
Usage guidance is explicit
Every entry specifies exact `use_for` and `do_not_use_for` constraints to prevent protocol authors from misapplying reference data.
Invariant 8
Access-controlled data never enters
Only openly licensed or public-use data is processed, ensuring all published aggregates can be freely audited without NDAs.
Invariant 10
An abstract is not the paper
Abstracts frequently omit exclusion criteria, rounding rules, or unweighted caveats. Full primary texts are required for inclusion.
